# K8: friction- and fit-robust insertion candidate, and the dry-run release (2026-11-15)

Sim only. Nothing here ran on the arm. No existing config, limit or weight changed. K4 (`runs/insert_K4.pt`) and K7 (`runs/insert_K7.pt`) are unchanged.

Inputs: `CONTACT_SENS_20261115.md` (port friction is the main sensitivity), `K7_20261114.md` (K7 fine-tune and its acceptance rule).

## Summary

- **No K8 candidate passes.** Three variants were trained (K8a, K8b, K8c). All three fix the friction and fit sensitivity of the contact grid (port mu 0.35-0.60: K7 53.0 % -> 91.8-93.8 %; tight plug: 92.1 -> 97.8-98.1 %; n = 1024 per cell). All three lose the start-gap cell `gap8` of the K7 set (K7 97.1 % -> 49.6-72.3 %, n = 1024). So no `runs/insert_K8.pt` and **no new config**. K4 stays on the arm; K7 stays the candidate of `K7_20261114.md`.
- The `gap8` loss grows with the high-friction training: K8b checkpoints 5M / 15M / 20M / 26M / 30M give 94.6 / 97.1 / 89.6 / 75.3 / 72.3 % on `gap8`, while port mu 0.35-0.60 rises (15M: 80.9 %, 30M: 93.8 %). In these runs, robustness to high friction and the 8 mm start trade off.
- The real K4 seat times (n = 14) fit sim port mu 0.20 or less (section 1). So the real port acts like a low-friction port **in this sim model**. The high-friction robustness of K8 may not be needed on the rig.
- Task B: in the sim dry runs the plug does not slide back at the jaw opening. The **retreat** pulls it out: a pad sphere of the open sim jaws (24 mm limit) touches the reader body and drags the plug 0.1-0.7 mm. Batched (n = 906): 28 % of nominal plugs lose full depth in the retreat at the chain's jaw opening, 0.1 % with the jaws at 27 mm. This is a sim artefact (section 3). Proposed dry-run option: `--release-open-m 0.027`.

- The real seat times fit the sim at port mu 0.20 or less (KS p 0.40-0.46). Every bin at 0.20 and above has a longer seat-time tail than the real runs (KS p <= 0.006). The seat count gives the same answer: 13 of 14 is unlikely at mu 0.30-0.40 (p = 0.10) and very unlikely above 0.40.
- The lateral path of the commanded TCP does not separate the bins. The real path is 152 mm (p50) against 120-174 mm in the sim; it is mostly policy chatter in the joint target (the real observation is noisier), not search. Do not use it.
- So the real port acts like sim mu 0.20 or less **in this contact model**. This does not prove that the real friction coefficient is low: the sim port has sharp, rigid edges and no lead-in; a real port with an entry chamfer or spring contacts can act like a low sim mu at a higher true mu. The sim can over-penalise friction. The logs cannot separate the two. The operator measurement in `CONTACT_SENS_20261115.md` (insertion and extraction force) is still the way to get the value.

## 2. Task A: K8 training

Launcher `scripts/eval/k8_train.py` (calls `train_bimanual.py`; the patches exist only in that process). Per-world plug fit: `scripts/eval/k8_fit.py`.

- From K7 (`runs/insert_K7.pt`, sha256 `9a6e23b2...`), K7's env_json unchanged (placement box PC +/-30 mm, +/-15 deg; mating force 0-10 N; same PPO settings; 30M steps, 4096 worlds, curriculum stage 1.5, thr 0.99). - Contact randomisation changes (in the process default `DEFAULT_CONTACT_DR`, recorded in the run's `config.json` as `contact_dr`):

- Plug fit per world (new): the scene holds 12 plug outlines in the nominal receptacle (8.34 x 2.56 mm); each world gets one (geom_dataid and the peg-slot geometry batched per world). Clearance per side: width 20 / 45 / 67.5 / 90 um x thickness 50 / 65 / 80 um. The seat test and the contact labels use each world's own plug edge.
  - Check of the per-world fit (`fitcheck_k7.json`, K7, base contact, n = 214-281 per plug): nominal 98.9 %, tight 20/50 um 91.8 %, loose 90/110 um 43.9 % seat with 90.2 % full depth. The whole-scene plugs of `CONTACT_SENS` gave 98.7 / 92.1 / 42.2 %. The per-world fit reproduces them.

### Verdict

No candidate. No `runs/insert_K8.pt`, no `bimanual_align10_all` variant with K8. Nothing for the operator to approve.

Next steps (server, sim only):
1. Find why `gap8` fails after high-friction training (trace the approach from 8 mm: does the policy descend faster and miss laterally?). A K8b fine-tune with a share of 8-10 mm start gaps in the training starts is the obvious fix to try.
2. Get the real friction value first (operator: insertion and extraction force, `CONTACT_SENS_20261115.md`). The real seat times say the rig acts like low sim friction; if the measurement agrees, K8 is not needed.


## 3. Task B: "not seated after release" in the dry run

### Test

`scripts/eval/k8_release_dry.py` runs `deploy.run_bimanual --dry-run` with sim-side options that exist only in that process (off by default; the deployed chain does not change): `--plug tight` (20 / 50 um), `--port-mu 0.3`, `--release-open-m X` (sim right finger limit from the first release tick), and a truth trace of every tick from the insertion to the end (depth, lateral, sim seat test, finger opening, hand-reader contacts). Config `bimanual_align10_all` (K4), `--stage full --align-mode full`, seeds 7041-7045, n = 1 per seed and condition. Summaries: `results/real_align/k8/release_dry_<cond>.json`, traces `runs/k8_eval/dry/`.

`scripts/eval/k8_release_probe.py` is the batched version: the contact_sens start and seat detector, then the chain's release order (0.4 s jaws open with the right target at the measured pose and the left press target at its measured pose; right TCP back 50 mm along the port axis at 20 mm/s; left hold 25 ticks; left hand up 30 mm). n = 1024 worlds per row, seed 8700, K4. Table `results/real_align/k8/release_probe.md`.

### Dry runs (n = 1 each; only runs with a seat detection reach the release)

| Condition | 7041 | 7042 | 7043 | 7044 | 7045 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| base (nominal plug, mu U(0.10, 0.20)) | press abort (3 of 3 runs) | in | in | align stop | **out** (3 of 3 runs: 5.06, 5.45, 4.90 mm) |
| port mu 0.3 | in (1 of 2 runs; other: guard bound) | align stop | in | in | **out** (5.54 mm) |
| tight plug | press abort | in | in | in | align stop |
| tight + mu 0.3 | align stop | in | guard bound | align stop | in (5.63 mm, -0.08 at the retreat) |
| jaws to 27 mm at the release | in | in | in | in | guard bound (run 1); in (run 2: 5.66 mm); full depth 5.61 mm but sim seat test false (run 3: 0.04 mm move at the opening) |

"in" = sim seat test true at the end of the release (depth >= 5.55 mm). The stops before the release (press abort, guard bound, align stop) are dry-run variation of the chain; they are not the release question.

### Where the plug comes out

- In every "out" run the depth stays constant while the jaws open (0.4 s), then the plug comes out by 0.1-0.7 mm **during the retreat**, about 40 ticks (0.8 s) into it. The jaws were already at their sim limit (24.6 mm per finger; grasp 19.0 mm). The trace with contacts (seed 7045, rerun 3) shows the contact: `left_pad_s13` (an end sphere of the left pad) touches `peg_34` (the reader body) when the plug starts to move. So the hand drags the reader out by a pad sphere; the plug does not slide back by itself.
- Batched probe (K4, n = 1024 per row): with the chain's jaw opening (0.024 m) the retreat takes the plug out of full depth in 257 of 906 nominal-fit worlds that were fully in after the opening (28 %). With the jaws past full open (27 mm) the retreat takes out 1 of 898 (0.1 %). Port mu 0.3 cuts the retreat loss to 42 of 701 (6 %); the tight plug to 38 of 638 (6 %); both to 2 of 260 (0.8 %).
- The jaw opening alone (before the retreat) takes 3 % of nominal plugs out of full depth (27 of 925), 1.4 % of tight (9 of 645), 12.5 % of loose (112 of 893). Depth change p50 0.01-0.03 mm. This is the small effect that `CONTACT_SENS` saw.

### Does the real plug move at the release?

- The real logs cannot see the plug after the jaws open (FK sees the hand; the wrist camera resolves about 0.8 px per mm along the port axis, `DEPTH_SPRINGBACK_20261114.md`).
- What they show: the session docs record the plug in after the seated real runs (10 of 10 on 2026-11-10/11, `CURRENT.md`; the 2026-11-13 runs left it in, with a 2 mm band on the force pushes); the load curve of the push has no slip step; a plug put 1-3 mm out by hand stays where it is; the real jaws are 4.1 mm wider than the grasp when the retreat starts (sim: 5.6 mm). No real run shows a plug pulled out by the retreat.
- So the retreat drag is a sim artefact: the sim pad model (4 x 4 spheres of radius 1.2 mm) and the sim hand pose after the release let an end sphere catch the reader body, and the low sim port friction does not hold the plug against it. Confidence: moderate (the real side has no plug measurement; n = 1 dry runs).
- The plug "sliding back in a loose, low-friction fit" is real in the sim but small (3 % nominal), and the CONTACT_SENS hypothesis "made larger by the retreat" is half right: the retreat is the main effect, through hand contact, not through fit.

### Proposed sim option for dry runs (not a changed default)

`k8_release_dry.py --release-open-m 0.027`: the sim right jaws open to 27 mm from the first release tick. Dry runs: every run that reached the release kept the plug at full depth (7041-7044, and 7045 in 2 of 2 reruns; one of those 7045 runs fails the sim seat test at 5.61 mm depth). Batched: retreat loss 0.1 % (n = 898). Use it when a dry run checks the release; keep the default (24 mm) when a dry run checks a change to the release itself, and read "not seated after release" there as the known sim drag. A second, weaker option is `--port-mu 0.3` (retreat loss 6 %, n = 701); it changes the insertion too (K4 seat 79 % at mu 0.3 in the probe, n = 1024), so it is not a neutral dry-run default.

These options live in the eval wrapper. Making one a `SimIO` option (e.g. `sim.release_open_m` in a config) is a one-line change in `deploy/bimanual/io_sim.py`, which another worker owns today; not done.

## Limits

- Sim only. The contact values are guesses. The friction fit (section 1) says how the real port acts in this sim model, not the real coefficient.
- Dry runs are n = 1 per seed and condition and vary between reruns (seed 7041: 3 press aborts with the base, a seat with other options). Use the batched probe for rates.
- The release probe emulates the chain's release order; it has no force push before the release.
- The thickness clearance above 80 um is not trained (seat-test artefact, section 2).
