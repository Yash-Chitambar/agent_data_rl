## Stationary RGB reference

`cameras/stationary_rgb_001/` contains a 15-second no-motion three-camera capture, host arrival times for every acquired frame, and every tenth raw image plus its calibrated 320×320 policy crop. `timing_summary.json` summarizes the capture. Observed host receive rates in this collector were approximately 29.7 Hz for ZED and 20.0 Hz for each wrist camera, although the wrist devices reported a 30 Hz setting. Those rates include driver, preprocessing and scheduling effects and must not be treated as intrinsic exposure rates. The collector does not run a policy: `policy_consumed_ns` is empty, wrist exposure timestamps are unknown, and ZED image timestamps are in an unaligned clock domain. Exact repeated frame hashes were zero in this capture; that is not proof of unique exposures.

High pickup approach `motion/right_pickup_high_empty_hold_001/` was stopped before the hold because measured joint 3 was 0.0291 rad below the requested value, exceeding the 0.020 rad attainment check. Using the active offsets and simulated forward kinematics, the requested TCP was approximately `[0.1780, 0.1440, 0.1800] m` and the reached TCP approximately `[0.1742, 0.1407, 0.1749] m` (about 7 mm apart). These are model-derived estimates, not external wrist measurements. The operator cleared the stop and reported the arm returned to rest. No hold samples were collected. Preserve this failed approach as controller-response evidence; investigate settling, gravity/friction and repeatability before attempting task-level pickup or changing gains.

The follow-up midpoint approach `motion/right_pickup_mid_empty_hold_001/` stopped at a 0.0275 rad joint-3 shortfall and returned to rest with torque off. Its `approach.csv` preserves 285 requested and executed targets with observation and CAN-send timestamps, and `encoder_right.csv` preserves native arrivals. All 285 commands had send timestamps and requested targets matched the executed targets. Joint 3 was roughly 43–44 mrad behind during the middle of the move; after the final command, native feedback remained at approximately 0.70439 rad throughout the observed one-second settling period, versus the 0.73192 rad target. Median send-after-request time was 2.89 ms (p99 4.88 ms). This is consistent with sticking or a load-dependent residual but does not establish the cause or calibrated torque. The high approach and midpoint were different poses, not repeats. Retain the failed trials; do not relax the attainment limit or change controller gains based on them alone.

`motion/right_pickup_mid_empty_hold_001/cpu_baseline_replay.{csv,json,png}` compares the native real encoders to a CPU MuJoCo replay of the recorded, safety-limited commands at their CAN send times. The current baseline tracked joint 3 more closely than the real arm, with 0.0266 rad RMS real/sim discrepancy over the overlapping feedback samples. The replay uses logged kp/kd, the existing scene's gravity compensation and default friction, and no contacts; it does not reproduce the real driver's complete gravity/friction feedforward. It is a diagnostic baseline, not an identified controller or held-out validation. Reproduce with `scripts/tools/replay_motion_cpu.py`.

`cpu_dev_friction_sweep.json` and the associated `cpu_dev_friction_*` artifacts compare constant MuJoCo joint-3 `frictionloss` values on that same midpoint approach. The 2.0 N m simulator parameter gave the lowest RMS among the tried values (5.69 mrad versus 26.62 mrad for the 0.01 N m baseline), but the final simulated joint remained 17.46 mrad away from the real encoder. This is an in-sample sensitivity study only. No candidate was adopted; the value is a simulator parameter, not a calibrated measured torque. The driver feedforward, timestep sensitivity, other joints/poses and held-out trials remain to be checked.

The same frozen 2.0 N m candidate was then compared with the separate folded-pose triangle in `motion/right_rest_empty_j3_triangle_001/cpu_dev_{baseline,friction_2}.{csv,json,png}`. RMS decreased from 4.37 mrad to 2.43 mrad, but the real joint spanned only 0.381 mrad while the candidate moved 7.54 mrad (baseline 14.72 mrad). The candidate therefore misses the observed sticking even though its RMS is lower. This is an exploratory cross-pose comparison, not a predeclared held-out gate; test a stateful-friction candidate and reserve complete future poses for validation.

An experimental CPU-only stick/slip torque-cancellation candidate (`--stiction 3 1` in `scripts/tools/replay_motion_cpu.py`) performed worse: joint-3 RMS was 14.45 mrad on the midpoint approach and 9.77 mrad on the folded triangle. On the folded triangle it moved 17.91 mrad versus the real encoder's 0.38 mrad. Outputs are `cpu_dev_stiction_3_1.{csv,json,png}` in both runs. This simple hysteresis approximation omits full coupled-constraint and driver feedforward behavior; it is rejected as a candidate and is not used by training or deployment. The 3/1 N m values are experimental simulator inputs, not calibrated torque measurements.

A second experimental version uses the inverse joint-space mass matrix to account for coupling while sticking (`--stiction 3 1 --stiction-mode coupled`). At 1 ms / 0.5 ms timesteps its joint-3 RMS was 14.659 / 14.654 mrad on the midpoint approach and 0.262 / 0.301 mrad on the folded triangle. Maximum difference between the two timestep traces was 0.066 mrad on the approach and 0.317 mrad on the folded triangle; the stick/slip state counts also changed substantially at the finer timestep. Outputs `cpu_dev_coupled_3_1_dt*` remain exploratory, use the same non-held-out recordings, disable contact and omit real driver feedforward and a full constrained-force solve. Do not promote this model; refine transition stability and validate on separate complete poses.
